Once upon a time, in a quiet village beside a wide blue river, a curious child found a little brass key.
The key was warm from the sun. It had no label, no ribbon, and no hint about the door it might open.
"I will find out," said Mira, and she tucked the key into her pocket.

She asked the baker, who shook flour from his sleeves. "I have a dozen doors," he said, "but none that small."
She asked the gardener, who was watering a row of bright green beans. "Try the old gate by the hill," he said.
So Mira climbed the hill. At the top stood a wooden gate covered in ivy, and in its center was a tiny brass lock.

The key turned with a click. Beyond the gate was not a treasure room, but a garden full of wildflowers.
Bees hummed above the clover. A narrow path led to a bench beneath an apple tree.
On the bench lay a book with a note: "For the person who was curious enough to look."

Mira carried the book back to the village. Each evening, she read a story aloud by the river.
Soon the baker came, then the gardener, then neighbors from every lane.
And whenever someone asked where the stories began, Mira smiled and said, "With a small question, and a key."

The river kept moving under the moon. The old gate stayed open, and the garden grew brighter every spring.
